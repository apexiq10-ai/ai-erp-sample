### A1 · Category convergence

**AI-native is table stakes. Win on proof at the customer's complexity.**

Every ERP now ships an assistant. AI in the product no longer separates anyone.

What still separates this AI-native ERP is architecture: agents inside a real-time ledger, and an evidence chain that never crosses a seam.

The hard objection: why replace the ERP at all? Because every seam between a ledger and the tools around it is a gap your auditor has to test.

### A3 · The ERP buying system

**Controllers create demand. Auditors grant permission. Sell to both.**

Controllers and CFOs create the pull; they live the reconstruction every month. None of them can move the general ledger alone.

The auditor, the audit committee, security and the implementation partner ask a different question: can we switch the system of record without risking a reporting period?

Exhibit: Who wants it, who permits it

Creates demand: Controller (A close without reconstruction) · CFO (Leverage and confidence) · Chief Accounting Officer (Defensibility) · Finance Systems (Fewer seams)

Permits the switch: External auditor (Testable evidence and a clean cutover) · Audit committee (No disruption to reporting) · Security and vendor risk (Access controls, data handling, AI use disclosed) · Implementation partner (A migration they can stand behind)

Both converge on: A signed migration

Implication: **Enthusiasm without auditor comfort buys evaluations, not migrations.**

### A4 · Parallel Close economics

**Run their last close. Tie it to the numbers they already signed.**

The prospect picks its last closed month. The AI-native ERP connects the sources, runs that close and ties it out to the results the controller already signed.

The test isn't speed. It's whether the team reaches the same signed numbers with far less reconstruction, and trusts it enough to do it again.

### A7 · PMM loops

**Build loops, not campaigns. Start with the signups that went quiet.**

A campaign ends. A loop reads a signal, makes a decision, acts, and learns from the answer, every cycle.

Three loops share one event stream: segmentation, go-to-market and reactivation. Reactivation comes first. A dormant signup already raised its hand, and the product data shows exactly where it stopped.

Rules first, models later. A rule an operator can read beats a score nobody can explain to a controller.

Exhibit: Three loops, one event stream

Plumbing: Product events and CRM activity land in the warehouse · Rules assign segment, stall point and reason to every account · A reverse sync writes them back to the CRM and the lifecycle tool · Every send, reply and session returns as an event

| Loop | Reads | Decides | Does | Owner | Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Segmentation | Incumbent ERP, entity count, billing model, funding stage, product events | Segment, fit tier and buying trigger | Routes the account to its message and play | PMM with RevOps | Weekly |
| Go-to-market | Message tests, channel touches, stage velocity | Keep, change or stop each play | Shifts the play mix and budget | PMM with Demand | Monthly |
| Reactivation | Where a signup stopped, and for how long | Why it stopped | Sends one matched play, timed to the close calendar | PMM with Lifecycle and Sales | Weekly |

The reactivation loop: Detect · Diagnose · Act · Measure · Learn

| Stalled at | Missing event | After | Likely reason | Matched play | Success event |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signed up | source_connected | 7 days | Integration effort | Integration walkthrough for Finance Systems | source_connected |
